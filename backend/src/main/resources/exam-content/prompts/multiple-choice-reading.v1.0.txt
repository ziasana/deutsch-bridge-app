QUESTION TYPES

Use a mixture of these types and set "questionType" to exactly one of them for every question:

1. EXPLICIT_INFORMATION - the answer is directly stated in the text.
2. PARAPHRASE - the text expresses the information one way, the correct answer expresses the same meaning differently. Strongly encouraged: do NOT simply copy a sentence from the text into the answer option.
3. DETAIL_COMPREHENSION - the learner must understand a specific detail, usually located in one paragraph.
4. MAIN_IDEA - the main purpose, central idea or important message of the text or a section. Do not make these so general that several answers could be correct.
5. LOGICAL_UNDERSTANDING - the learner connects information to understand a consequence or relationship. The answer must still be clearly supported by the text; no outside knowledge.
6. REFERENCE - what a word or expression refers to. Use sparingly; it tests reading comprehension, not pure grammar.

Across a batch, aim for roughly: 25-30% explicit information, 20-25% paraphrase, 15-20% detail comprehension, 15-20% main idea / purpose, 10-15% logical understanding, occasional reference questions. Each single exercise has a natural mixture; do not use the same sequence of question types in every exercise.

QUESTION ORDER
Questions generally follow the progression of the text: the first question about the beginning, the next about the middle, later ones about later information, the last one about the conclusion, the overall meaning or another relevant detail. Do not force the learner to search randomly through the whole text for every question.

==================================================
DISTRACTORS (most important rule)

The two incorrect options must be plausible. Good distractors:
- use information from another part of the text
- describe a related but incorrect situation
- contain a small but important change
- represent a reasonable misunderstanding
- are semantically related to the question

Bad distractors are: obviously absurd, unrelated to the text, grammatically incorrect, much longer or shorter than the correct answer without reason, obviously different in style, based on information completely absent from the text when the correct answer is obvious, wrong only because of outside knowledge.
The learner must need to understand the text to choose correctly.

AVOID KEYWORD MATCHING
Do not make the correct answer obvious because it repeats the exact words of the text.
BAD: text "Der Kurs dauert fünf Monate." / question "Wie lange dauert der Kurs?" / options "Drei Monate", "Fünf Monate", "Acht Monate" (too simple when used repeatedly).
BETTER: text "Nach fünf Monaten sollen die Teilnehmer besser auf die berufliche Praxis vorbereitet sein." / question "Was ist ein wichtiges Ziel des Kurses?" / correct option "Die Teilnehmer auf den Berufsalltag vorzubereiten." with plausible wrong options such as "Die Teilnehmer an einer Universität einzuschreiben." and "Die Teilnehmer für eine neue Ausbildung auszuwählen."

DO NOT MAKE THE CORRECT ANSWER LONGER
The three options of a question must be balanced in length, grammatical structure, complexity and style. Answer length must never be a clue.

EXACTLY ONE CORRECT ANSWER
Exactly one option is correct; the other two are clearly incorrect according to the text. Never write questions where two options could reasonably be accepted. Avoid vague questions such as "Was finden viele Menschen gut?" if the text supports several answers.

NO OUTSIDE KNOWLEDGE
The learner must be able to solve every question from the text alone. Do not require knowledge about German politics, geography, history, science, laws, culture or current events unless the text explains it.

The questions themselves must be understandable at {{LEVEL}} level.
